# Frequently Asked Questions

## General Questions

### Q: What is the duration of a master thesis?
**A:** The typical duration is one semester (4-6 months). The exact timeframe will be agreed upon with your co-supervisors during the kickoff meeting.

### Q: How many theses will be accepted?
**A:** A maximum of **six theses** will be accepted in this program. Each TA has their email pool randomly drawn, with one thesis selected per TA (up to three TAs).

### Q: Will I be co-supervised?
**A:** Yes. Every selected participant will be co-supervised by the program coordinator and their assigned TA.

### Q: How much time should I expect to dedicate to my thesis?
**A:** Full-time work is expected. Plan for 40+ hours per week, with the exact distribution depending on your specific project.

## Application Process

### Q: What's the difference between Path A and Path B?
**A:** 
- **Path A:** You select from a TA's pre-defined topic list and email them your application
- **Path B:** You propose your own idea to all TAs, have optional discussions ("affinity conversations"), and confirm with one interested TA

### Q: How do I submit my application?
**A:** Your application is a **single email** sent to **one TA** by the deadline, proposing **one thesis topic**. No other form is required.

### Q: Can I apply for multiple topics or TAs?
**A:** No. You must send exactly **one email** to **one TA** proposing **one thesis topic** by the **October 15, 2026** deadline. After this, do not send additional applications.

### Q: What should my email include?
**A:** Your email should include:
- A clear subject line mentioning the thesis topic
- Your motivation and why you find the topic interesting
- Relevant background, skills, or prior experience
- Any specific questions or ideas you have

### Q: What if I'm interested in Path B (self-proposed)? Do I need to email all TAs first?
**A:** For Path B, you may discuss your idea with multiple TAs first (these are "affinity conversations" to confirm fit). However, your **formal submission** is a single email to **one TA** confirming they are willing to supervise your proposed topic.

### Q: What if multiple TAs show interest in my Path B proposal?
**A:** You should confirm with the TA whose research most closely aligns with your interests and expertise. Once you send your confirmation email to one TA, your application is locked to that TA and topic. Do not send additional applications.

### Q: Do affinity conversations need to happen before I send my email?
**A:** For Path B, yes. "Affinity conversations" with interested TAs should happen before you send your final confirmation email. These discussions help both you and the TA confirm you're a suitable candidate for co-supervision.

For Path A, you can simply email the TA with your application—no prior discussion is required, though you may contact them with questions.

### Q: What happens after I send my email?
**A:** After you submit your email by the deadline, you will not receive individual confirmation that your application was received. The random draw will be conducted after the **October 15, 2026** deadline, and you will be notified of the results by **November 1, 2026**.

### Q: Is there a minimum GPA or prerequisite?
**A:** Specific prerequisites may vary by topic. Check the topic descriptions or contact the faculty advisor directly.

## The Random Selection Process

### Q: How does the random draw work?
**A:** After the **October 15, 2026** deadline:
1. Each TA independently receives and collects all emails proposing topics for them
2. Each TA conducts a **publicly verifiable random draw** from their pool of received emails
3. One participant is selected per TA
4. The selected participant is paired with that TA on their proposed topic

### Q: How is the random procedure "publicly verifiable"?
**A:** The exact mechanism will be announced before the deadline. The method is designed so that all participants can independently verify that the selection was fair and truly random (e.g., using seeded random number generators, cryptographic hashing, or public randomness sources).

### Q: What are my chances of being selected?
**A:** Each email in a TA's pool has an equal probability of being randomly selected. Your chances depend on how many total emails that TA receives. If 12 people email a TA, each has a 1-in-12 chance of being drawn.

### Q: What if I don't get selected?
**A:** Unfortunately, only six theses total will be accepted (one draw per TA, up to three TAs). If your email isn't drawn, you won't be paired with a TA for this program. You're welcome to discuss future opportunities or alternative arrangements directly with the TAs.

### Q: What if I believe there was an error in the random procedure?
**A:** Contact the program coordinator immediately. Because the procedure is publicly verifiable, any error can be detected and corrected. However, the random selection itself cannot be appealed—only procedural errors can be addressed.

### Q: Can I increase my chances of being selected?
**A:** Your chances depend on the size of the TA's email pool, which you cannot control. The best strategy is to propose a strong topic and align with a TA whose research matches your interests—but once the deadline passes, selection is random.

## During the Thesis

### Q: How often do I meet with my co-supervisors?
**A:** This will be negotiated with your co-supervisors during the kickoff meeting, but typically weekly or bi-weekly meetings are recommended. You may also have additional meetings as needed.

### Q: What if I want to change my thesis topic?
**A:** Contact your co-supervisors as soon as possible. Topic changes are possible early in the process but become more difficult as the thesis progresses.

### Q: Can I conduct research abroad?
**A:** Possibly, depending on the nature of your research and your co-supervisors' approval. Discuss this at your kickoff meeting.

### Q: What are the expectations for progress?
**A:** You will have a midterm presentation (**January 15, 2027**) to demonstrate progress. Your co-supervisors will provide specific milestones for your project.

## Submission & Defense

### Q: What is the final submission deadline?
**A:** **April 15, 2027** - Late submissions may result in grade penalties.

### Q: What format should my thesis follow?
**A:** Guidelines will be provided by your co-supervisors and the program coordinator. Standard academic formatting is expected.

### Q: When is the thesis defense?
**A:** Defense dates are in May 2027 (**May 1-31, 2027**). Specific dates will be scheduled in consultation with your defense committee and all parties involved.

### Q: How is the thesis graded?
**A:** Grading is based on:
- Quality of research and methodology (40%)
- Originality and innovation (25%)
- Writing and presentation (20%)
- Completion and deliverables (15%)

## Support & Resources

### Q: Is there training on academic writing?
**A:** Yes, the university writing center offers workshops and individual consultations. Your co-supervisors may also recommend resources.

### Q: What if I'm struggling with my thesis?
**A:** Contact your co-supervisors immediately. We also have peer mentoring and additional support services available.

### Q: Can I get an extension?
**A:** Extensions are only granted in exceptional circumstances and require approval from the program coordinator and your co-supervisors. Request early if needed.
